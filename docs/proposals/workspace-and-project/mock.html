Workspace and Project

One project, a tree of design data with variations and a history. One workspace, a flat 2-D surface of stations looking into it, each at a level of detail that sets both its size and how much it computes. A lifecycle over both, from sketchbook to refinement, that decides how strict everything is. The domain here is fabric because it was the richest example you gave.

What the surface is said

  • Flat and 2-D, like an artboard, not docks and tabs. Ordinary HTML and CSS, no canvas-based scene.
  • Many stations at once, forty is not strange, sized very unevenly on a large monitor.
  • A station is a view into the project, not a node of it. It may read several nodes or a slice of one.
  • Not everything is a station. Plain text and HTML sit on the surface as annotations and guideposts to what you are looking at.
  • A workflow carries a set of views, possibly in sequence. A view is an arrangement: which stations, where, how big.
  • Layout follows rules that cut down the choices while keeping the page calm. A rule need not fill the screen: a mostly blank page with a few small stations is a real way to plan what comes next, what kind it is, and how big.
  • Size and detail decide each other in both directions, with some back and forth: a rule proposes a size, a view can pin a rung, a station can ask for more.
  • Leaders can connect a piece of one station to a piece of another. Early in the lifecycle they are free-form and carry no type; later they are refined into real relations. Both matter.

What the mock decided guess

  • A rule hands out area; a station picks the rung that fits; a view may override either. The Plan rule shows the sparse case with a dashed slot for the next station.
  • Guideposts are saved with the view, in the workspace layer, never in the project.
  • Clicking promotes a station to hero and demotes the old hero one rung, instead of swapping places.
  • The compute dot has three states: cached (icon), light (card), live (full). Nothing runs for a station that is off the view.
  • A station is of a kind (weave, palette, render, analysis, queue) and the kind owns its three renderings. Kinds would be the reusable library pieces.
  • The Fibonacci rule names five stations by weight 5 : 3 : 2 : 1 : 1 and sweeps the rest into a tray.

3 · the project

A tree with two axes of change: history and variation

Versions run along time and cover the whole project. Variations sit side by side at one moment and cover one node: three twills under one name, each a candidate. A sweep is a product over variation sets. A spike picks one or two candidates and carries the rest of the design forward in a way that may never work for the others. said variation is first-class and probably distinct from history. deferred whether a "current" candidate exists at all, and whether it lives in the project or in the workspace; the prior-art list below has four systems that each answer this differently.

The tree said

  • A hierarchy of heterogeneous data: text, images, structured parts, whatever the domain needs.
  • The interface to the outside world. Parts of it go to a simulator, a renderer, a compiler, a printer; results come back into it.
  • Safe concurrent editing, undo, versioning. Edits arrive from a human, from an agent through the workspace's tools, from an agent on the filesystem, from several agents at once.
  • Backing stores vary: a git repository of files first; a plain directory, browser storage, or S3 are all plausible.
  • Built for one person. Agent-friendly primitives first; human chrome only where a human needs it, and no chrome built in early.

Decided for the mock guess

  • Derived nodes (cloth, drape) are in the tree as nodes with a recipe, so a result always names the inputs and the version it came from.
  • The render queue is a node too: jobs out, results back, each stamped with the project version.
4 · the lifecycle

Sketchbook to refinement, and back again by markup

A design moves through phases, and the phase sets how strict everything is: how hard things are linted, which states a station may be in, whether a leader must have a type. The phases are the same whether the project is a fabric, an analog circuit, a mechanism or a theorem. You can always go backwards: regress a design, fork it, mark up a finished design the way you would mark up a blank page, then merge the markup and the design into a new mock. The humans rough out; the agent implements in a principled way; that exchange is itself history worth keeping, and it is not git history. said all of it. guess the five phase names, and that lint has three levels.

Roles said

You are the design lead: you judge, prompt, direct. The agent is the technical architect and the engineering staff: it decides how and does the work. The affordances we take from older systems were made for a builder's own hands; the ones we design are for a judge's eyes and a director's voice.

The kitchen is the working analogy and we will not push it. Stations are worked by the staff. The chef stands at the pass and inspects every plate before it leaves; a plate that fails goes back to its station. The expediter keeps the order of the tickets. The pass is the review point the lifecycle needs; the tickets are the workflow's sequence.

Flows to plan for said

  • Forward: sketchbook, mock, first build, measurement, refinement, each stricter than the last.
  • Regress: take a full design back to a mock by marking it up, keeping the design.
  • Merge: markup plus full design becomes a new mock, then a new build.
  • Fork: a spike on one variant that may leave the others behind.
  • Record: the rough-out and the principled implementation that answered it, kept as the design's own history.
5 · writers and watchers

Many hands on one store, and two kinds of reload

The workspace is one writer among several and must notice changes it did not make. Code changes come through the dev server as today. Content changes come through the store's own change feed, whatever the store is. said both kinds exist and must stay separate. deferred conflict resolution: last write per node, locks, or an eventually consistent store for real-time joint editing are all open. guess the change feed looks like a version counter plus a list of touched paths, the same shape over git, a directory watcher, or S3.

6 · what is saved where

The project is what the world reads; the workspace is how you look at it

Both are versioned and both are worth sharing, but an offline tool should never have to wade through arrangements and levels of detail to get at the design. said the split matters and the usual binary (one workspace file beside everything else) may be too coarse. guess a middle grain: a workspace folder in the same repository, with one file per view, so views can be diffed, reviewed and shared one at a time; and a lifecycle record of its own beside both.

How to read them

  • Name the organizing principle in one sentence before describing the UI.
  • Say whose hands it served, and whether the principle survives when the hands are an agent's.
  • Note what it stored where: content, arrangement, history, picks.
  • Note where it failed, if it did, and whether the failure was the principle or the chrome.
8 · where the questions stand

Settled for now, deferred, and newly open

Settled for now said

  • Station is the word. Kitchen names are welcome, lightly.
  • Size and detail decide each other both ways, with iteration.
  • Layout need not fill the surface; a sparse planning arrangement is a real view.
  • Text and HTML guideposts live on the surface beside stations.
  • Leaders are both: free-form early, typed later, and the refinement is itself recorded.
  • The lifecycle is a first-class concept that gates strictness and available states, and runs backwards as well as forwards.
  • You are the design lead; the agent is the architect and the staff.

Deferred until built deferred

  • The shape of a variation set, whether a current pick exists, and whether it lives in the project or the workspace.
  • Conflict resolution between writers: last write, locks, or eventual consistency.
  • The exact phase names and what each gates.

Newly open

  1. Is the phase a property of the whole project, or can one node be in mock while its neighbour is refined?
  2. Is a phase a gate with written criteria, or a label you move at will?
  3. Where do markups live: ink and notes in the workspace layer, or nodes in the project the agent must answer?
  4. Does a spike become a branch on the history axis, a variant on the variation axis, or both at once?
  5. What does the agent need from a view to do its work: the whole arrangement, or only the nodes in focus and the guideposts?
